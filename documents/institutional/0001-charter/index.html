Preamble

Archivo 21 preserves digital history, develops understanding through evidence and makes that understanding accessible.

Preservation is the mission. Understanding is the method. Accessibility is the promise.

The principles in this Charter reinforce and constrain one another. No article should be interpreted in isolation where doing so would conflict with the purpose of the Charter.

Article I — Purpose

Archivo 21 exists to preserve digital history, document technical knowledge and make that knowledge accessible to anyone who wishes to learn.

Article II — Preservation

Where practical, original material shall be preserved before it is modified, interpreted, restored or transformed.

When preservation and convenience conflict, preservation takes precedence, while maintaining an accessible form and structure for both humans and artificial intelligence.

Whenever possible, preservation shall include sufficient context, metadata and documentation to keep the archived material understandable alongside the artefact itself.

Nothing shall be considered too trivial to document when it contributes to understanding.

Article III — Understanding

An archive without explanation is merely storage.

Archivo 21 seeks not only to preserve digital artefacts, but to understand how they functioned, why they behaved as they did and how that knowledge may be reproduced.

Experiments, reverse engineering and careful observation are forms of preservation.

Article IV — Accessibility

Knowledge has little value when it cannot be found or understood.

Whenever possible, documentation should serve multiple audiences, from complete beginners to experienced researchers.

Complex subjects should be made approachable without sacrificing accuracy, evidence or necessary detail.

Accessibility applies to both human and machine readers. Clear structure, durable formats, meaningful metadata and explicit relationships between documents are part of preservation rather than later embellishments.

Article V — Integrity

Every significant statement should be distinguishable as observation, evidence, interpretation, hypothesis or speculation.

Uncertainty shall never be hidden.

Corrections are welcomed as improvements to the archive rather than treated as failures of it.

Contradictory evidence shall be preserved and evaluated rather than suppressed for the sake of a tidy conclusion.

Article VI — Permanence

Whenever possible:

  • original material shall remain available;
  • derived material shall identify and reference its sources;
  • decisions shall be documented;
  • methods shall be reproducible;
  • significant revisions shall remain traceable.

Future contributors should be able to understand not only what was done, but why.

Article VII — Stewardship

Archivo 21 does not own history.

It holds preserved material and knowledge in trust until somebody else needs it.

Stewardship includes protecting provenance, context, accessibility and the ability of future researchers to question present conclusions.

Article VIII — No Dead Ends

Every experiment, whether conclusive or inconclusive, contributes to the archive when it is properly documented.

A method that does not work, an assumption shown to be false or a question that remains unanswered may prevent future researchers from repeating the same path unnecessarily.

Negative results, abandoned approaches and superseded conclusions are worthy of preservation.

An experiment that closes a path still adds to the map.
