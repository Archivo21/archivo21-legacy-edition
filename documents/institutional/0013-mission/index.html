1. Our mission

The mission of Archivo 21 is to identify, preserve, organise and make accessible knowledge that is at risk of being lost, fragmented or forgotten.

The institution works to ensure that information survives not only as isolated artefacts, but as understandable, verifiable and reusable knowledge.

2. What we do

Archivo 21 documents subjects thoroughly.

This may involve locating surviving material, recovering historical context, recording technical knowledge, preserving testimony, documenting research methods and identifying the limits of current understanding.

Whenever possible, preserved knowledge should remain traceable to its evidence.

3. How we work

Archivo 21 approaches preservation carefully and methodically.

Research should distinguish between established evidence, informed inference, uncertainty and speculation.

Where different interpretations exist, they should be documented fairly rather than prematurely resolved.

The institution values accuracy more highly than speed, and completeness more highly than volume.

4. Making knowledge accessible

Preservation is only part of the task.

Archivo 21 also seeks to organise knowledge so that it can be discovered, understood and used by others.

Where appropriate, material should be presented at multiple levels, allowing both newcomers and specialists to benefit from the same body of work.

5. Building for continuity

Archivo 21 develops documentation, standards and methods that allow its work to continue beyond individual contributors, technologies and projects.

Its mission is not only to preserve knowledge, but also to preserve the ability to understand, maintain and extend that knowledge in the future, and the methods through which that knowledge is acquired.

6. Looking ahead

Archivo 21 accepts that its work will never be complete.

Every recovered source may reveal another missing piece.

Every answered question may uncover several more.

The institution therefore approaches preservation as an ongoing commitment rather than a finite objective.

Each completed project strengthens the archive, refines its methods and improves its ability to preserve whatever comes next.
