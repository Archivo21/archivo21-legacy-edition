Purpose

This standard gives experiments a stable identity and a comparable structure without forcing related work into adjacent chronological numbers.

It standardises structure, not thought.

Experiment Identifiers

Every recorded experiment receives one permanent chronological identifier:

EXP001
EXP002
EXP003

The identifier records archival sequence, not subject, importance, success or relationship.

Once assigned, an experiment identifier should not be reused or renumbered.

Related experiments should not be represented through improvised suffixes such as EXP001a or EXP001-2.

Series IDs

Related experiments may belong to one or more Series.

A Series ID groups work by subject or continuing question while each experiment retains its permanent chronological EXP### identifier.

Example:

Experiment: EXP001
Series: Foundations

A later foundation experiment may be EXP019 or EXP244 and still belong to the same Series.

A Series record should contain:

  • stable Series ID;
  • title;
  • purpose or scope;
  • member experiments;
  • relevant open questions;
  • current summary of findings;
  • revision history.

The exact Series ID syntax and the rules for membership in multiple Series remain open for ratification.

Experiment Status

Status records where the work is in its lifecycle. Working values are:

  • Proposed
  • Planned
  • In Progress
  • Concluded
  • Superseded

Status is separate from outcome.

Experiment Outcomes

Do not classify an experiment as a failure merely because its expected result did not occur.

Working outcome terms are:

  • Answered
  • Partially Answered
  • Unanswered
  • Inconclusive
  • Superseded

A disproven hypothesis may still produce an answered experiment. A method shown not to work may be a useful negative result.

Question

State the question narrowly enough that the result can be evaluated.

Why This Is Interesting or Noteworthy

Explain why the observation, claim or technique merits investigation.

An experiment does not need to prove that it is necessary in order to deserve preservation.

Background

Summarise relevant prior knowledge, community claims, documentation and related experiments.

Assumptions

Record what was believed or provisionally accepted before testing.

Materials and Environment

Record relevant software, versions, operating system, hardware, mods, tools, files, neighbourhoods, save states and configuration.

Procedure

Describe exactly what was done in a reproducible order.

Use concise references for routine prerequisites rather than repeating their full explanation.

Observations

Record what was directly observed without folding interpretation into the description.

Evidence

List and preserve screenshots, video, logs, files, savegames, hashes and other supporting material.

Distinguish source material, preservation copies and access copies where applicable.

Analysis

Interpret the observations and compare them with the question, assumptions and prior knowledge.

Conclusion

State what the experiment answered and what remains uncertain.

Unexpected Discoveries

Record noteworthy results outside the original question.

Reproducibility

Record:

  • preparation required;
  • specialist knowledge required;
  • time sensitivity;
  • risk to data or environment;
  • recovery requirements;
  • approximate effort;
  • known sources of variation.

The final Archivo 21 reproducibility scale and its cultural reference names remain open for ratification.

Future Work

Identify follow-up experiments, unresolved variables and useful independent verification.

Related Experiments

List related EXP### identifiers even when they are not numerically adjacent.

Revision History

Record material changes to the method, evidence, analysis, outcome or evidence level.

Negative and Inconclusive Results

A negative or inconclusive result must remain documentable.

The experiment record should explain what was ruled out, what could not be determined and which conditions were actually tested.

This implements Charter Article VIII: No Dead Ends.
