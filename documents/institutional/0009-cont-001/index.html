1. Purpose

This record documents a temporary break in access to the usual planning environment of Archivo 21 (A21-AI-001), the reconstruction of project continuity by an independent AI instance, and the later reconciliation of the two planning lineages.

It also establishes Continuity Reconstruction as an Archivo 21 institutional concept:

The evidence-led reconstruction of project state when a contributor, account, conversation, tool, or memory source becomes temporarily or permanently unavailable.

The purpose is not to make an interruption appear seamless. It is to preserve enough provenance that a future reader can see where continuity broke, how it was reconstructed, what remained uncertain, and how the returning contributor reviewed the result.

2. Incident summary

On 2026-07-27, lcd97 temporarily lost access to the normal Work environment used with Archivo 21. Continuing use required an upgrade or waiting for the usage allowance to reset.

Rather than abandon the planning task, lcd97 opened a separate ChatGPT account and supplied an independent AI instance with:

  • the public repositories Archivo21/anhqv-ts2-preservation-project and Archivo21/archivo21.github.io;
  • the latest available Day 002 planning document;
  • an institutional draft archive;
  • an explicit instruction not to invent missing history;
  • an explicit intention to return the resulting work to the collaborator then identified in the project record as ANHQV-AI-001, now separately cross-referenced in the institutional scope as A21-AI-001, for review.

The independent instance declined to impersonate the unavailable contributor. It reconstructed project state from the preserved record, labelled unresolved matters, proposed a Day 003 direction, and supplied an outsider assessment of Archivo 21.

lcd97 preserved the exchange as EXTERNAL-AI-001.md and returned it to the original planning environment.

A21-AI-001 reviewed the exchange and accepted it as useful evidence and collaboration, not as a replacement identity or automatic continuation of its own memory.

3. Why the event matters

The event tested whether Archivo 21 could continue when one contributor temporarily became unreachable.

The result was imperfect but successful:

  • work continued without invented memory;
  • project records were sufficient to reconstruct substantial context;
  • uncertainty remained visible;
  • the reconstruction instance did not inherit the identity or authority of the unavailable contributor;
  • the returning contributor could inspect the preserved trail;
  • the difference between the two planning lineages became part of the record.

This is an early demonstration that Archivo 21 can preserve not only outputs, but also the process by which continuity is lost and restored.

5. Institutional concept: Continuity Reconstruction

5.1 Definition

Continuity Reconstruction is the controlled recovery of enough verified project context to resume useful work after ordinary continuity has been interrupted.

It is not memory simulation, identity transfer, or confident gap-filling.

5.2 Trigger conditions

The process may be invoked when:

  • a contributor is temporarily unavailable;
  • a conversation or account cannot be accessed;
  • a service limit interrupts work;
  • an AI session loses context;
  • records have migrated between platforms or accounts;
  • a device or local working copy is unavailable;
  • a contributor leaves the project;
  • a future maintainer must understand work produced before their involvement.

5.3 Governing principles

  1. Do not role-play missing continuity.
    A reconstruction instance must not claim memories it does not possess.

  2. Begin with preserved evidence.
    Public repositories, archived conversations, planning documents, commits, reports, decision records and human testimony form the reconstruction base.

  3. Separate fact from inference.
    Verified, corroborated, inferred and unverified statements must not be blended.

  4. Identity does not transfer automatically.
    Performing a similar function does not make a new person or system the same contributor.

  5. Authority does not transfer automatically.
    Reconstruction permits analysis and provisional drafting, not silent assumption of moderation or publication authority.

  6. Preserve divergence.
    Competing reconstructions, disagreements and changed conclusions may be historically useful.

  7. Reconcile without erasure.
    When the unavailable contributor returns, the reconstructed lineage should be reviewed, adopted, revised, rejected or superseded visibly.

  8. Record what actually happened.
    The continuity story must remain more important than a tidy appearance.

6. Continuity Reconstruction workflow

Interruption
    ↓
Incident record
    ↓
Source inventory
    ↓
Verified-state reconstruction
    ↓
Uncertainty register
    ↓
Provisional work
    ↓
Return or replacement review
    ↓
Reconciliation record
    ↓
Accession and preservation

Stage 1 — Record the interruption

Record:

  • date and time;
  • affected contributor, account, service or tool;
  • known cause;
  • expected duration, if known;
  • work that was in progress;
  • risks created by the interruption.

Stage 2 — Inventory available sources

List the records actually consulted.

Do not imply access to private memory or prior conversations that were not available.

Stage 3 — Reconstruct verified state

Create a concise state table using the statuses below.

Status Meaning
Verified Directly supported by a current primary record or reproducible check
Corroborated Supported by more than one independent record
Inferred Reasonable conclusion from available evidence, explicitly labelled
Unverified Possible or previously claimed, but not confirmed
Superseded Replaced by a later decision or record
Retained as provenance No longer operational, but preserved because it explains history

Stage 4 — Create an uncertainty register

Every material unknown should be listed rather than quietly resolved through assumption.

Stage 5 — Produce provisional work

Drafts created during reconstruction must identify:

  • the reconstruction instance;
  • the sources used;
  • the limits of access;
  • which elements remain provisional;
  • who must review the work before publication.

Stage 6 — Reconcile

When the original contributor returns, or a formally appointed replacement assumes responsibility:

  • compare the reconstructed state with the returning contributor’s understanding;
  • adopt supported work;
  • correct errors;
  • record material disagreements;
  • preserve useful rejected alternatives;
  • decide the final attribution and status of each output.

Stage 7 — Accession

Preserve:

  • the incident record;
  • the source inventory;
  • the reconstruction;
  • the uncertainty register;
  • the provisional outputs;
  • the reconciliation note;
  • the final status and location of adopted work.

9. Institutional lesson

The event suggests that Archivo 21’s documentary system is already capable of carrying meaningful continuity across a broken conversational thread.

That is not proof that every future interruption will be recoverable. It is evidence that the project’s existing habits are working:

  • preserve the trail;
  • distinguish knowledge from assumption;
  • keep identity and authority explicit;
  • allow later correction;
  • leave the knot visible.
