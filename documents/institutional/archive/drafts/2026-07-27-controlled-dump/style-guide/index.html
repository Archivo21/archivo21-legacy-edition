Archivo 21 Style Guide

Status: Draft 0.1
Canonical status: Not yet institutional canon

Purpose

This guide defines the institutional writing culture of Archivo 21. It governs clarity, structure and treatment of evidence without enforcing conclusions or erasing individual voice.

1. The Reader Comes First

Write so that the least experienced intended reader can enter the subject without being patronised.

An expert loses little from a clear explanation. A beginner loses everything when necessary context is assumed.

2. Every Claim Must Earn Its Place

Prefer description over promotion.

Write:

This technique changes the foundation height.

rather than:

This amazing trick completely transforms foundation building.

Avoid adjectives and certainty that the evidence has not earned.

3. Explain Before Instructing

Instructions should state what an action does, not merely which control to press.

Write:

Open the cheat console with Ctrl + Shift + C; this provides the command line used by the following procedure.

rather than:

Press Ctrl + Shift + C.

A durable explanation survives interface changes better than an unexplained sequence of clicks.

4. Record What Happened

Distinguish observed results from expected results.

Write:

On the tested installation, the game regenerated the neighbourhood terrain.

rather than:

This should regenerate the terrain.

Record versions, dates, configurations and relevant conditions.

5. Preserve the Journey

Do not document only the successful solution.

Preserve dead ends, incorrect assumptions, unexpected discoveries, abandoned approaches and superseded methods when they help explain the final understanding or prevent repeated work.

6. Separate Evidence from Interpretation

Use explicit labels where useful:

  • Observed
  • Confirmed
  • Evidence
  • Interpretation
  • Hypothesis
  • Speculation
  • Open Question

A reader should not have to infer how certain a statement is.

7. Humour Is Welcome

Archivo 21 may sound human and may be funny.

Humour must not obscure instructions, evidence, provenance or risk. A joke may accompany an explanation; it may not replace one.

8. Write for the Future

Assume that somebody may read the page twenty years later without the present context.

Prefer:

Version 1.3.5, tested 27 July 2026

over:

the latest version, tested recently

Dates, versions, names and links should be explicit enough to survive time.

9. One Primary Idea per Page

A page should have one clear purpose.

Large subjects may be divided into focused pages and connected through indexes, series pages and cross-references. Division should improve navigation, not scatter one explanation into fragments that cannot stand alone.

10. Archive Before Beautifying

Preserve raw material before cleaning, compressing, editing or interpreting it.

Where possible, distinguish:

  1. original or source material;
  2. preservation copy;
  3. access copy;
  4. edited or derived presentation;
  5. interpretation.

Presentation must not silently replace provenance.

11. Respect the Reader's Time

Complete documentation need not force experienced readers to reread routine explanations.

When a lengthy beginner explanation can be represented by a concise paragraph, table, checklist or reference entry, provide that concise form for experienced readers and link it to the full explanation.

A recurring action should be explained properly once and referenced thereafter. For example:

Prerequisite: Open the cheat console (Ctrl + Shift + C).

Do not repeatedly reproduce the same long explanation merely to make each page longer.

Layer documentation so that newcomers can learn and experienced readers can proceed quickly.

12. Avoid Sterile Writing

Documentation should be clear, precise and natural.

Avoid corporate filler, academic padding, formulaic transitions and inflated language that add words without adding meaning.

Prefer:

The game regenerates the terrain.

over:

It is important to note that the game will subsequently proceed to regenerate the terrain.

Humans and artificial intelligence are both capable of natural or sterile writing. The problem is sterility, not authorship.

Write as though explaining the subject to an intelligent colleague rather than attempting to impress a committee.

13. Every Page Should Be Worth Finding

A reader arriving through a search result or direct link should be able to understand why the page exists.

Every page should contain enough context to stand on its own while linking to prerequisites, deeper explanations and related work.

No dead pages. No orphan knowledge.

14. Standardise Structure, Not Thought

Archivo 21 standardises how knowledge is organised and communicated, not which conclusions contributors are expected to reach.

Contributors may question assumptions, propose alternatives and publish contradictory findings when they distinguish observation from interpretation and support their conclusions with appropriate evidence.

Standards exist to make knowledge comparable, reproducible and understandable.

They do not exist to enforce consensus.
