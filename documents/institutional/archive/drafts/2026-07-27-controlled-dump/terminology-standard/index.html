Archivo 21 Terminology Standard

Status: Draft 0.1
Canonical status: Not yet institutional canon

Preamble

Terminology exists to improve understanding, not to police language.

The preferred terms below support consistent indexing and comparison. Historical, community and informal language remains worthy of preservation and may be used when context requires it.

Core Terms

Behaviour

Anything a system demonstrably does, regardless of whether it was intended.

Use this as the neutral default when intent is unknown.

Intended Behaviour

Behaviour supported as deliberate by documentation, developer statements, source material or sufficiently strong evidence.

Defect

Behaviour demonstrated to contradict intended functionality.

Do not infer a defect merely because a result is inconvenient or surprising.

Side Effect

A secondary result produced by another action.

A side effect may be beneficial, harmful or neutral.

Limitation

A constraint inherent to the tested system, method or environment.

A limitation is not necessarily a defect.

Quirk

A consistent, unusual behaviour that is not inherently incorrect.

Exploit

A technique that intentionally uses behaviour outside the expected design to achieve a result.

The term describes use of the behaviour; it does not by itself imply harm.

Myth

A commonly repeated claim for which the available evidence does not provide support.

A myth may remain historically or culturally important and should not be erased.

Legend

A long-standing community claim whose truth remains unresolved.

A legend is neither confirmed nor dismissed merely because it has been repeated for a long time.

Resolved Legend

A former legend that has acquired enough evidence to be classified more precisely.

Its prior status and the path to resolution should remain documented.

Canon Terms

Canon

Information treated as authoritative within a defined context and supported by the evidence relevant to that context.

The context must be stated when ambiguity is possible.

Community Canon

Information broadly accepted within a community.

Community Canon may be accurate, incomplete, interpretative or later disproven. Its social acceptance is itself part of the historical record.

Archive Canon

Information accepted by Archivo 21 following evaluation under the Evidence Standard.

Archive Canon is provisional. It may be revised when stronger, broader or contradictory evidence becomes available.

An Archive Canon statement should identify its evidence level and the scope within which it applies.

Appendix A — Deprecated Terminology

This appendix aids interpretation of historical and community material. It does not forbid contributors from quoting, discussing or understanding the language used by others.

Historical or broad term Preferred Archivo 21 term Reason
Bug Behaviour or Defect Bug may imply unintended behaviour before intent has been established. It remains acceptable informally or in quotations.
Glitch Behaviour, Defect or Quirk The term is too broad for precise indexing without additional description.
Corruption Data corruption, neighbourhood corruption, file corruption, or another specific form State what data or structure is affected.
Impossible Not currently known; not reproduced; unsupported under tested conditions Avoid universal claims that exceed the tested evidence.
Broken Defective, incomplete, inaccessible, incompatible, or another specific condition Describe the actual condition and scope.
Failed experiment Inconclusive, unanswered, partially answered, or a documented negative result An experiment may produce useful knowledge even when the expected result does not occur.

Deprecated terms are contextualised rather than erased. Their historical use may remain essential to searchability, community history and provenance.
