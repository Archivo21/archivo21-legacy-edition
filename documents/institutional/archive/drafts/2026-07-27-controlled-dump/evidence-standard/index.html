Archivo 21 Evidence Standard

Status: Draft 0.1
Canonical status: Not yet institutional canon

Purpose

The Evidence Standard communicates how strongly a claim is supported.

Evidence levels describe confidence, not importance.

An anecdotal observation may begin a major discovery. An established result may confirm something ordinary. The scale never measures cultural value, research interest or preservation priority.

Evidence Ladder

The descriptive name is primary. The codes shown here are working shorthand and remain subject to final ratification.

E0 — Anecdotal

A claim, recollection or report exists, but Archivo 21 has not directly observed it under documented conditions.

It may originate from community discussion, personal memory, inherited notes or an undocumented report.

It is worthy of preservation and investigation, but should not support a firm conclusion by itself.

Confidence: Unverified.

E1 — Observed

The result has been directly observed and supporting evidence has been preserved.

It has not yet been reproduced through a documented procedure, or it has been observed only once.

Confidence: Initial.

E2 — Reproduced

The result has been produced again using a documented methodology.

The tested conditions and relevant environment are recorded sufficiently for the repetition to be evaluated.

Confidence: Moderate.

E3 — Independently Verified

Another researcher has reproduced the result using the published documentation.

Independent verification should identify deviations, missing assumptions and environmental differences rather than merely state agreement.

Confidence: High.

E4 — Established

The result has been repeated across multiple relevant environments, versions, configurations or independent researchers.

No unresolved contradictory evidence currently undermines the stated scope.

Established does not mean permanently unquestionable.

Confidence: Very high within the documented scope.

ER — Retired

A formerly accepted conclusion has been disproven, superseded or shown to apply only under narrower conditions than originally stated.

The retired conclusion, former evidence level, reason for retirement and replacing understanding shall remain preserved.

Confidence: No longer current.

Applying a Level

Every evidence assessment should identify:

  • the claim being assessed;
  • the level and descriptive name;
  • the date of assessment;
  • the evidence and experiments relied upon;
  • the tested scope;
  • known limitations or contradictions.

Example:

Evidence status: Reproduced (working code E2)
Scope: The Sims 2 Ultimate Collection under the documented test configuration
Assessed: 27 July 2026

Change over Time

Evidence levels may rise or fall.

A reproduced result may later be narrowed by contradictory evidence. An anecdotal report may become independently verified. A retired conclusion remains part of the archive because the history of an error may explain later discoveries.

Changing an evidence level is a documented revision, not an embarrassment.

Relationship to Canon

Community Canon records acceptance by a community.

Archive Canon records Archivo 21's evaluated position.

Neither term replaces an evidence level. Archive Canon statements should carry a current evidence classification and a clearly stated scope.

The minimum evidence threshold, if any, for promotion to Archive Canon remains an open institutional question.
