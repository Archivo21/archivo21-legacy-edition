Acquisition Policy

1. Purpose

Archivo 21 acquires material in order to support preservation, documentation, research and future access.

Acquisition is not an end in itself.

Material should be acquired because it contributes meaningfully to the institution’s ability to preserve or understand a subject, not merely because it is available.

2. Scope

Archivo 21 may acquire or archive original material, copies, derivatives, contextual documentation, testimony, technical evidence and other records relevant to its work.

The institution may also acquire knowledge about material it cannot itself obtain. This may include its existence, location, provenance, known characteristics or conditions of access, provided that the information can be documented responsibly.

Acquisition decisions should remain consistent with the institution’s mission, values, capacity and current areas of work.

3. Relevance

Material should have a clear relationship to an existing project, collection, research question or recognised preservation need.

Archivo 21 may acquire material whose importance is not yet fully understood when there is a reasonable possibility that it could support future research or prevent irreversible loss.

Relevance should be interpreted broadly enough to preserve context, but not so broadly that collections lose coherence.

4. Preservation priority

Priority should be given to material at genuine risk of loss, degradation, inaccessibility or disappearance.

Risk may arise from physical fragility, technological obsolescence, restricted access, platform closure, neglect, undocumented ownership, dispersal or dependence on a single surviving copy.

Preservation priority should be determined through documented judgement rather than a fixed ranking system.

Material should not be treated as endangered merely because immediate acquisition is convenient.

5. Evidential value

Acquired material should strengthen the surviving evidence relating to a subject.

This may include material that confirms existing understanding, challenges it, complicates it, fills a gap or preserves the boundary of what remains unknown.

Contradictory, incomplete or apparently insignificant evidence should not be excluded solely because it does not support an established interpretation.

6. Provenance and documentation

Wherever practical, Archivo 21 should record where acquired material came from, how it was obtained, what is known about its history and whether any uncertainty remains.

Material with incomplete provenance may still be acquired when its preservation value justifies doing so.

In such cases, the limits of what can be established should be recorded clearly.

7. Authenticity and verification

Archivo 21 should assess acquired material carefully enough to distinguish between confirmed, probable, uncertain, altered, reconstructed and misidentified material.

Unverified material may be preserved when it has research value, but it should not be presented with greater confidence than the available evidence supports.

Suspected alteration, fabrication or misidentification should be documented rather than silently corrected or discarded where the record of that uncertainty may itself be useful.

Sources and contributors should be described respectfully. Necessary critical assessment should address the evidence without needless disparagement of the person or source from which it came.

8. Duplication

Archivo 21 may acquire duplicate material when duplication improves security, comparison, verification, accessibility or preservation.

Duplicates should not be accumulated without purpose.

Where several copies exist, meaningful differences between them should be documented where practical.

9. Rights, restrictions and responsibilities

Acquisition does not automatically grant Archivo 21 the right to reproduce, publish, modify or distribute material.

Known ownership, copyright, privacy, access and usage restrictions should be recorded and respected.

Where the lawful or ethical status of material is unclear, the acting governance should determine access and use case by case. Restriction may be appropriate while uncertainty is examined, but should not operate as an automatic default.

The institution should avoid acquiring material where possession or use would create obligations it cannot responsibly meet.

10. Ethical acquisition

Archivo 21 should not knowingly acquire material obtained through theft, coercion, deception, exploitation or unauthorised access.

The institution should consider the likely effect of acquisition, preservation and publication on living people, contributors, communities and legitimate rights holders.

Preservation value does not remove the need for ethical judgement.

11. Capacity and proportion

Archivo 21 should ordinarily acquire material only when it can reasonably preserve, document and manage it.

A smaller body of well-understood material is preferable to a larger accumulation that cannot be organised, verified or maintained.

The urgency of an acquisition may justify temporary rescue preservation before complete processing capacity is confirmed.

After careful review, material that Archivo 21 cannot feasibly process should be transferred or redistributed responsibly to a more suitable custodian rather than retained indefinitely in institutional backlog.

12. Refusal and deferral

Archivo 21 may refuse, defer or redirect an acquisition when the material falls outside its mission, lacks sufficient relevance, presents unresolved legal or ethical concerns, duplicates existing holdings without adding value or exceeds the institution’s current capacity.

Refusal does not imply that the material lacks value.

Where practical, Archivo 21 may privately record the existence of significant material it cannot accept or direct it towards another suitable custodian.

13. Review, reassessment and transfer

Acquisition decisions may be revisited as new evidence, improved methods, changing risks or increased capacity alter the institution’s understanding.

Material previously considered peripheral may later become central.

Material already acquired may require reclassification, restricted access, additional documentation or transfer to a more appropriate custodian.

Where material is transferred, Archivo 21 should preserve a local record, reference or copy wherever lawful and appropriate.

Reassessment should preserve the history of the original decision where that history remains relevant.
