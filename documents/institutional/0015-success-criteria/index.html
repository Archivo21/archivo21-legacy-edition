1. Purpose

Archivo 21 succeeds when knowledge that might otherwise have been lost remains available, understandable and capable of supporting future work.

Success is not measured solely by the amount of material collected. It is measured by the quality, durability and usefulness of what has been preserved.

2. Preservation

A preservation effort is successful when the surviving evidence has been identified, retained and documented as fully as circumstances reasonably allow.

Original sources should be preserved wherever practical.

Where original material is unavailable, surviving derivatives, testimony, technical evidence and contextual information should be gathered carefully enough to support future examination.

3. Understanding

Preserved material should not remain an unexplained accumulation of files, fragments or observations.

A successful project provides enough context for others to understand what the material is, where it came from, why it matters to the subject being documented and how its parts relate to one another.

Claims, interpretations and reconstructions should remain distinguishable from the evidence on which they depend.

4. Traceability

Knowledge preserved by Archivo 21 should remain traceable to its sources wherever possible.

A reader should be able to determine which evidence supports a conclusion, which reasoning connects the evidence to that conclusion and where uncertainty remains.

When claims cannot be fully verified, the limitation should be recorded rather than obscured.

5. Accessibility

A successful archive can be found, entered and navigated by the people for whom it may be useful.

Material should be organised clearly and presented at suitable levels of detail.

Specialist depth should remain available without making introductory understanding unnecessarily difficult.

Accessibility also requires practical consideration of formats, tools and technologies that may otherwise prevent future use.

6. Continuity

A project succeeds more fully when its work can continue without depending entirely upon the people who began it.

Its organisation, methods, terminology and unresolved questions should be documented well enough for another contributor to understand the existing work and proceed responsibly.

Where a process is important to the reliability of the archive, that process should be preserved alongside its results.

7. Reliability

Archivo 21 should be able to recognise and explain the reliability of its own work.

Evidence should be assessed carefully.

Contradictions should be acknowledged.

Corrections should be incorporated without concealing the history of earlier understanding where that history remains relevant.

A successful archive does not present confidence more strongly than its evidence permits.

8. Proportion

The depth of preservation should reflect the nature of the subject, the evidence available and the realistic capacity of the institution.

Thoroughness does not require every project to become indefinitely large.

A successful project establishes an appropriate scope, documents significant omissions and avoids expanding so widely that its central purpose becomes unclear.

9. Completion and continuation

Archivo 21 may declare a project complete when its defined purpose has been fulfilled to a documented and defensible standard. Completion is a procedural status within a bounded scope and does not imply that all possible interpretations, extensions, or conceptual branches related to the subject have been exhausted.

Completion does not imply that nothing more can ever be discovered, or that a concept cannot be expanded upon any further.

A completed project should remain open to correction, extension and renewed investigation when new evidence or better methods become available.

10. Institutional progress

Archivo 21 succeeds as an institution when each project leaves it better able to preserve the next.

Its work should contribute not only preserved knowledge, but also improved methods, clearer standards, stronger infrastructure and a more accurate understanding of its own limitations.

Progress may therefore be found in recovered material, resolved questions, documented uncertainty, refined processes and lessons drawn from unsuccessful approaches.
