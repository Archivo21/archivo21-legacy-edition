1. Purpose

Archivo 21 exists to preserve, organise and make accessible knowledge that might otherwise become fragmented, inaccessible or permanently lost. Through careful documentation, evidence-based research and long-term stewardship, it seeks to ensure that future generations can reconstruct, understand and build upon the knowledge preserved within its archives.

2. Why preservation matters

Knowledge does not disappear only when every copy of an original source is destroyed. It can also be lost gradually through neglect, technological change, broken links, discontinued services, inaccessible formats, undocumented practices, incomplete recollections and the dispersal of evidence across many separate places.

Archivo 21 seeks to reduce the likelihood that information disappears before it can be documented, understood and shared, regardless of how trivial, obscure or insignificant it may appear at the time.

The present cannot reliably predict what the future will consider useful, revealing or worthy of study. Information dismissed today may later provide evidence of how people lived, communicated, created, worked, played or understood the world around them.

3. Preservation without premature judgement

Archivo 21 does not require knowledge to prove its importance before preserving it.

The institution recognises that cultural, historical, technical and scholarly significance may emerge only after the original context has changed or disappeared. Preservation therefore comes before final interpretation.

Archivo 21 seeks to document evidence faithfully while distinguishing between what is known, what is inferred, what is uncertain and what remains unresolved.

It does not preserve information in order to impose a single authoritative interpretation upon it. It preserves enough evidence for future researchers, communities and individuals to examine that information for themselves.

4. Comprehensive preservation

Archivo 21 values comprehensive preservation over extensive acquisition.

The institution would rather preserve a smaller body of knowledge thoroughly than accumulate a larger collection of incomplete, disconnected or poorly understood material.

Comprehensive preservation may include original sources, surviving derivatives, technical evidence, historical context, testimony, competing interpretations, uncertainties, failed approaches and the methods used to reach conclusions.

Where complete reconstruction is impossible, Archivo 21 seeks to preserve the boundaries of present knowledge as carefully as the knowledge itself.

An honest record of what could not be recovered is preferable to a confident reconstruction unsupported by evidence.

5. Accessibility and understanding

Preservation is incomplete when knowledge survives but cannot be found, accessed or understood.

Archivo 21 therefore seeks not only to retain information, but to organise and explain it in forms that remain usable beyond the circumstances in which it was collected.

Its archives should help different audiences enter the material at different levels. A person seeking a simple explanation should not be forced through specialist documentation, while a researcher seeking technical depth should be able to examine the underlying evidence.

Accessibility does not require the removal of complexity. It requires that complexity be navigable.

6. Evidence and uncertainty

Archivo 21 is committed to evidence-based research.

Sources should be preserved wherever possible, claims should be traceable to their evidence, and uncertainty should be stated rather than concealed.

The institution recognises that archival work often involves incomplete, damaged, contradictory or inaccessible material. In such cases, it seeks to document the available evidence, the reasoning used and the limits of any conclusion reached.

Corrections, revisions and new discoveries are not threats to the archive. They are part of its continuing development.

7. Long-term stewardship

Archivo 21 approaches preservation as a responsibility extending beyond the duration of any individual project, platform, technology or contributor.

Its work should be organised so that others can understand, maintain, verify, migrate and continue it.

The institution seeks to avoid unnecessary dependence on knowledge held only in private memory, inaccessible systems or undocumented processes.

Preserved knowledge should remain capable of surviving changes in personnel, technology and institutional circumstance.

8. Participation

Archivo 21 should make it possible for people with different levels of time, knowledge and technical ability to contribute meaningfully.

Structure should make preservation easier, not participation harder.

Standards, procedures and documentation should improve the reliability and continuity of the archive without creating unnecessary barriers to involvement.

Contributions may vary in size. A major reconstruction, a corrected citation, an eyewitness recollection, a technical observation or the recovery of a single surviving file may each become an important part of a wider preservation record.

9. Institutional continuity

Archivo 21 is intended to outlast its founders, its first projects and the technologies through which it was created.

Its identity should not depend entirely upon a single person, platform, collection or field of study.

The institution may change its methods as better methods become available, but its commitment to careful preservation, accessible knowledge, evidence and long-term stewardship should remain recognisable.

Archivo 21 preserves not only for those who participate in it now, but for people it will never meet.
