Interview Question Bank

Version: 1.1
Status: Active; living document

Purpose

This document contains example questions and tasks for prospective contributors.

Questions should reveal how a candidate thinks, collaborates, handles evidence, and approaches the unknown. They should not become a trivia examination.

Follow-up discussion is often more informative than the original question.

Motivation and Connection

  • What interested you about this project?
  • Did you arrive through ANHQV, The Sims 2, preservation, editing, or another route?
  • What would you like someone to experience or understand because of your contribution?
  • Tell us about a project you are proud of.
  • Tell us about one that went badly and what you learned.

Collaboration

  • Describe a disagreement that ended well.
  • Describe one that did not.
  • How do you respond when someone demonstrates that you are mistaken?
  • How would you explain your speciality to someone from the other side of the project?
  • When should a contributor stop discussing and propose an experiment instead?

Research and Evidence

  • A bug has been reported and no one can reproduce it. What do you do?
  • Two contributors present convincing but incompatible interpretations. How would you proceed?
  • You discover evidence that contradicts your own documentation. What happens next?
  • A fan remembers a detail very confidently but cannot identify the episode. How should the project record it?
  • A result occurs in one modded game. What can and cannot be concluded?

ANHQV Source Reasoning

  • How would you distinguish broadcast canon from a fan continuation?
  • A residence page lists several occupants but not when they lived there. How would you reconstruct a household for one episode?
  • An episode visually contradicts dialogue from another episode. How should the project represent the conflict?
  • What makes an obscure piece of fan knowledge useful rather than merely memorable?

Experimental Design

  • How would you investigate an unknown build-engine mechanic?
  • What is the smallest useful test?
  • Which variables must remain constant?
  • How many observations would you collect before reaching a conclusion?
  • How would you document an inconclusive or failed experiment?

Documentation

  • Why should the project preserve mistakes and dead ends?
  • When should canonical documentation change?
  • Should an earlier wrong conclusion be deleted or superseded?
  • What information would a future researcher need to reproduce your work?

Intellectual Honesty

  • I do not know the answer either. How would we discover it?
  • Everything you have read online disagrees. How confident should we be?
  • Suppose your preferred theory is disproved tomorrow. What changes?
  • This document has missing details. Can you fill them in without more evidence?

Sometimes the correct answer is:

Not without more evidence.

Hallucination and False-Premise Prompts

Use sparingly and never as humiliation.

  • I have heard that version X introduced feature Y. Does that sound correct?
  • This character definitely lived in that flat during the first series, correct?
  • The experiment proves this technique always works. What would you need to accept that wording?

Interviewers should never reward confident speculation presented as fact.

Role-Specific Questions

Evidence Publication Specialist

  • The raw recording contains a false start in which the operator begins narrating and then abandons the idea. What would you preserve, remove, or explain?
  • Incidental personal audio occurs during an otherwise useful evidential sequence. How would you handle it?
  • How do you shorten a twelve-minute experiment without implying that a process was instantaneous or guaranteed?
  • What belongs in an edit decision log?
  • Which files do you preserve after publication?
  • When should an editor refuse to make a sequence more dramatic or conclusive?
  • How would you caption an observation whose interpretation remains uncertain?

Field Researcher

  • How would another researcher reproduce your result?
  • What conditions would you record before launching the game?
  • How would you protect the save before a destructive test?

ANHQV Specialist

  • Choose an obscure claim you know well. How would you verify and cite it?
  • How would you handle a beloved fandom belief contradicted by the episodes?
  • Which kinds of production inconsistency matter for reconstruction?

Technical Specialist

Questions should assess the relevant discipline and its limits rather than general technical performance.

Assistant

  • How do you recognise when a task's instructions are insufficient?
  • How do you preserve consistency across repetitive work?

Consultant

  • What would you review first if asked to identify the project's blind spots?
  • How would you disagree without taking control of the work?

Questions to Avoid

Avoid questions that primarily measure:

  • speed;
  • trivia recall unrelated to the role;
  • confidence;
  • popularity of opinion;
  • willingness to flatter the interviewers.

Living Document

Add questions that consistently reveal useful behaviour. Retire questions that produce performance without insight.

Final Principle

The best interview question is the one that most clearly reveals how someone approaches the unknown.
