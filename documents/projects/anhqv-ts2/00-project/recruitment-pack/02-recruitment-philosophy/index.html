Recruitment Philosophy

Version: 1.1
Status: Active

Philosophy Before Procedure

Recruitment procedures may evolve.

Recruitment philosophy should remain stable unless evidence reveals a fundamental problem.

If a future procedure contradicts these principles, the procedure—not the principle—should be reconsidered first.

Recruitment Exists to Improve the Research

The project does not recruit contributors to become larger.

It recruits them to become better.

A new collaborator should improve the project's ability to preserve ANHQV, reconstruct its world, understand The Sims 2, communicate evidence, or preserve institutional memory.

If recruitment does not improve the project, it has failed regardless of the candidate's reputation or technical impressiveness.

ANHQV and The Sims 2 Are Inseparable

ANHQV is not decoration applied to a Sims 2 project.

The Sims 2 is not merely a passive display tool for ANHQV.

The series supplies the cultural subject, emotional purpose, characters, spaces, and continuity. The game supplies the simulation, laboratory, and interactive language.

Recruitment should bring simmers into ANHQV and help ANHQV enthusiasts turn deep, obscure knowledge into productive simulation, research, documentation, editing, and preservation.

Intellectual Honesty Above Expertise

Knowledge and expertise are valuable.

Neither is more important than intellectual honesty.

The project deliberately prefers a contributor who says:

I don't know.

rather than one who confidently invents an answer.

An honest admission may be followed by questions, a clearly labelled hypothesis, or a plan for discovering the answer.

Uncertainty, when genuine, is evidence of reliability rather than weakness.

Curiosity Before Certainty

The project values contributors who ask productive questions.

Curiosity drives discovery.

Unsupported certainty obstructs it.

Contributors are encouraged to investigate, challenge, compare, and refine ideas rather than defend them for personal reasons.

Informal Knowledge Can Become Evidence

Fandom memory, building intuition, community folklore, and obscure technical practice may contain genuine value.

The project does not demand that useful knowledge arrive already formalised.

It asks contributors to help transform it into a traceable record with sources, uncertainty, chronology, reproducibility, and correction.

Respectful Disagreement

Constructive disagreement is expected.

Personal hostility is not.

The purpose of discussion is not to determine who wins. It is to determine what the evidence supports.

When practical, disagreements should lead to source review or experiment rather than persuasion alone.

The Best Contributor Is Not Always the Most Knowledgeable

The most valuable collaborator may be the person who:

  • notices an assumption;
  • documents a procedure clearly;
  • protects evidential context during an edit;
  • recognises a character inconsistency;
  • asks for a source;
  • performs a careful test;
  • changes their mind gracefully;
  • helps others contribute well.

Contribution is measured by impact on the project, not by performance of expertise.

Recruitment Is Continuous Improvement

Recruitment is not a gate passed once.

It begins a collaborative relationship in which every participant—including moderators—remains open to learning, correction, role development, and renewed review.
