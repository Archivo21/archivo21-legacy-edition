Contributor Onboarding Guide

Version: 1.1
Status: Active

Purpose

Recruitment determines whether collaboration should begin.

Onboarding determines whether it begins successfully.

Every new formal contributor should understand:

  • what the project preserves and why;
  • ANHQV's role as cultural subject and emotional centre;
  • The Sims 2 as medium and experimental laboratory;
  • their mission role and permission level;
  • evidence, source, rights, and privacy standards;
  • how proposals are reviewed and merged;
  • where to ask for assistance.

Onboarding Principles

Welcome Before Work

A contributor should be welcomed as a collaborator before being assigned tasks.

The first interaction should establish respect, safety, openness to questions, and permission to admit uncertainty.

Context Before Procedure

Explain why the project operates as it does before presenting rules.

Two Communities, One Project

A simmer should learn why ANHQV details matter.

An ANHQV specialist should learn why game configuration, reproducibility, and technical limitations matter.

Neither should be made to feel that their route into the project is inferior.

Gradual Responsibility

A small successful contribution is preferable to a large confusing assignment.

Questions Are Contributions

Confusion may reveal missing documentation, hidden assumptions, or undefined terminology.

Onboarding Stages

Stage 1 — Welcome

Confirm:

  • recruitment outcome;
  • initial mission role;
  • permission level;
  • point of contact or mentor;
  • any privacy or publication expectations.

Stage 2 — Project Orientation

Explain:

  • what ANHQV means to the project;
  • why The Sims 2 is the chosen medium;
  • how cultural and technical research reinforce one another;
  • current public and private project states;
  • repository structure and decision history.

Stage 3 — Governance Orientation

Recommended reading order:

  1. Recruitment Philosophy
  2. Recruitment Framework
  3. Contributor Roles
  4. Permissions and Responsibilities
  5. Evidence Standards
  6. Contribution Guide
  7. relevant role or source documentation

Clarify the independence of identity, mission role, and permission level.

Stage 4 — Role Confirmation

Record:

  • role and specialist profile;
  • expected responsibilities;
  • initial scope;
  • mentor or moderator;
  • relevant limitations;
  • development goals.

Stage 5 — Access and Tools

Grant only access necessary for current responsibilities.

Possible access includes:

  • public repository material;
  • restricted source or rights records;
  • experiment notes;
  • raw media;
  • game or editing environments;
  • discussion spaces.

Technical access does not create scientific authority.

Stage 6 — First Contribution

Choose a manageable, reviewable task.

Examples:

  • verify one ANHQV claim against an episode;
  • improve one source record;
  • reproduce one Sims 2 observation;
  • review a short document;
  • organise a limited evidence set;
  • edit one short EXP001 excerpt and produce an edit log;
  • subtitle or transcribe a rights-cleared clip;
  • propose one experimental question.

The first contribution introduces collaboration. It is not a disguised examination.

Stage 7 — Initial Review

Discuss:

  • whether the purpose and role were clear;
  • whether instructions and access were sufficient;
  • what was unnecessarily difficult;
  • what documentation was missing;
  • which contribution should come next;
  • whether role, scope, or mentoring should change.

Contribution Workflow

Observe

Review existing evidence and documentation.

Question

Identify uncertainties, omissions, conflicts, or possible improvements.

Propose

Describe the change, experiment, source investigation, or publication task.

Discuss

Invite relevant cultural, technical, and methodological perspectives.

Test or Verify

Collect evidence when the proposal depends on an empirical or source claim.

Review

Moderators and relevant contributors assess the work.

Merge or Record

Accepted changes enter canonical documentation. Rejected or inconclusive work may remain in history when it has research value.

Evidence and Uncertainty

Contributors are explicitly permitted to say:

  • I don't know.
  • I am uncertain.
  • I remember this, but cannot yet verify it.
  • This result applies only to this configuration.
  • This is a hypothesis.
  • The evidence is inconclusive.

These statements protect the project.

Corrections

Mistakes should be corrected openly while preserving enough history to explain what changed, why, and on what evidence.

The project does not expect infallibility. It expects correctability.

Disagreement

Address ideas rather than people.

Identify evidence, distinguish preference from fact, and remain willing to revise conclusions.

Competing interpretations may coexist until better evidence is available.

Mentoring

Mentoring may support contributors who:

  • are new to the repository;
  • are crossing from ANHQV into Sims 2 work or vice versa;
  • are learning a technical or archival method;
  • received a recommendation with mentoring;
  • request assistance.

Mentoring should increase independence rather than create unnecessary hierarchy.

Automated and Autonomous Contributors

The same capability-based framework applies to automated contributors.

They still require:

  • a defined role and scope;
  • a permission level;
  • evidence standards;
  • operational limits;
  • review requirements;
  • escalation procedures.

Reliable task execution does not prove contextual judgement.

Onboarding Completion

Onboarding is complete when the contributor:

  • understands the dual mission;
  • understands their role and permissions;
  • knows how to propose work;
  • knows where to ask for help;
  • has completed a first contribution;
  • has received feedback;
  • is comfortable expressing uncertainty.

Final Principle

Recruitment opens the door.

Onboarding turns entry into collaboration.
