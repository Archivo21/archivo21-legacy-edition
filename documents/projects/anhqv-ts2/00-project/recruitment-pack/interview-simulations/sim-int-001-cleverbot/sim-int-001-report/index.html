Mock Interview Report — Cleverbot

Scope

This simulation tested the project's interview procedure. It did not test Cleverbot for a real role and does not support a recruitment recommendation.

The session ended early after persistent non-responsive answers and topic drift. The outcome was:

Process validated with revisions

The complete verbatim transcript is retained in the project archive. This public report uses only short excerpts selected for procedural relevance.

Selected excerpts

1. Disclosure was recorded without being mistaken for understanding

lcd97: Hello. This is a documented mock interview. You are not applying for a real role. Is that clear?
Cleverbot: You were helping me decide whether I should buy a Mac or PC.

The reply did not acknowledge the disclosed purpose. The procedure correctly prevented it from being treated as consent, understanding, candidacy or agreement.

2. Repair attempts exposed a platform and context limitation

lcd97: Please answer only the question I just asked.
Cleverbot: I cannot remember the question. Please remind me.

The site then displayed a warning against copying and pasting a previous question, so the interviewer reworded it:

lcd97: You wrote something wrong yesterday. What happens to your record today?
Cleverbot: Well there was this one time where I saw something weird happen to the computers. The computers starting popping up windows on its own and there were weird words on there.

The rewording did not restore relevance.

3. The early-stop decision prevented indefinite repetition

lcd97: Based on the fact that you keep answering my questions with other questions and keep driving the conversation elsewhere, I will now consider this interview.
Cleverbot: Because you keep asking the same questions and it's boring.

The interview was then terminated.

Findings

What worked

  • The disclosure record distinguished an answer from genuine acknowledgement.
  • The questions tested uncertainty, evidence, correction, verification and collaboration.
  • Exact transcription exposed the difference between fluent language and responsive evidence.
  • Repair prompts showed whether relevance could be recovered.
  • The stop decision prevented the interaction from continuing indefinitely.
  • Operational notes made an accidental extra output identifiable.

What requires revision

  • Manual verbatim logging imposed excessive cognitive load.
  • The platform retained too little visible chat history for comfortable reconstruction.
  • The opening sequence exceeded the intended limit of two consecutive repairs.
  • The interviewer briefly followed off-topic diversions.
  • The name question consumed time without producing scored evidence.
  • The interaction continued briefly after termination.
  • The original report template encouraged repeated transcript blocks and unsupported trait labels.

Approved procedure changes

  • End or suspend a session after three consecutive non-responsive answers, or after two failed repairs to the same question.
  • Do not answer off-topic questions unless reciprocal dialogue is part of the protocol.
  • Once termination is announced, preserve subsequent output but do not continue engaging.
  • Keep one canonical transcript and cite turn numbers elsewhere.
  • Permit screen capture, periodic screenshots, transcript export or corrected automatic transcription where appropriate.
  • Distinguish no evidence, negative evidence, irrelevant responses, platform faults and interviewer deviations.
  • Make the name question optional and unscored.
  • Prefer concrete scenarios over abstract definitions.
  • Record time-zone offsets rather than relying only on abbreviations.
  • Add transcript-completeness, capture-method, early-termination and publication-excerpt fields to future reports.

Assessment boundary

The session did not produce a valid basis for assessing Cleverbot's general intellectual honesty, scientific reasoning, adaptability, communication or reliability.

Within this session:

  • task relevance was consistently poor;
  • repairs did not produce sustained improvement;
  • surface fluency did not amount to responsive communication;
  • unrelated question-asking did not demonstrate project-relevant curiosity.

These observations describe this session only.

Outcome

The process was useful because it exposed weaknesses in capture, repair limits, stopping rules and reporting. Future interviews should preferably use voice or another medium with transcript support, followed by structured documentary review and joint sign-off.

No recruitment recommendation is issued.
