Recruitment Framework

Version: 1.1
Status: Active

Purpose

This framework establishes a consistent, transparent, and evidence-driven process for recruiting contributors to the ANHQV-TS2 Preservation Project.

It describes who should join, why they should join, how they are evaluated, and what standards govern recruitment.

This framework applies only to ANHQV-TS2 project participation. It does not recruit people into Archivo 21 as an institution, assign authority over other Archivo 21 projects, or create institutional financial or legal roles.

Project Mission

Recruitment serves a dual cultural and technical mission:

  1. Preserve, study, and transmit Aquí no hay quien viva as a cultural work and shared body of knowledge.
  2. Reconstruct and investigate its world through The Sims 2.
  3. Connect ANHQV specialists with simmers, builders, modders, editors, archivists, and technical researchers.
  4. Convert informal fandom knowledge into documented, testable, reusable project knowledge.
  5. Let people experience ANHQV through the videogame uniquely suited to speaking to them personally.
  6. Document how The Sims 2 works and how it was modified, exploited, or broken to make the reconstruction possible.

A recruitment process that improves only the technical side while forgetting ANHQV has failed. A process that preserves cultural knowledge but cannot turn it into a usable reconstruction or durable record is incomplete.

Recruitment Principle

We recruit collaborators, not authorities.

No contributor is recruited because they possess status, reputation, credentials, confidence, or perceived expertise.

A contributor is recruited because they improve the quality, reliability, accessibility, or sustainability of the project.

Authority is never assumed.

Evidence is.

Mission Statement

The purpose of recruitment is not to increase the number of contributors.

It is to increase the quality of the project.

Growth is not an objective by itself.

Improvement is.

Capability Before Implementation

Useful ideas may originate from any contributor regardless of:

  • experience;
  • profession;
  • reputation;
  • academic background;
  • technical expertise;
  • nationality or language;
  • whether they arrived through ANHQV fandom or Sims 2 practice;
  • implementation, whether human, language model, agent, or future autonomous system.

Incorrect ideas may also originate from any of them.

Contributors are evaluated by capability, reasoning, intellectual honesty, evidence handling, and willingness to revise conclusions.

Roles remain stable as implementations change.

Recruitment Objectives

Every recruitment decision should improve at least one of the following:

  • ANHQV source knowledge and cultural interpretation;
  • reconstruction fidelity;
  • Sims 2 experimental capability;
  • documentation quality;
  • audiovisual communication;
  • preservation and rights handling;
  • technical knowledge;
  • critical review;
  • accessibility and translation;
  • institutional memory;
  • project sustainability.

If none improves, recruitment should not proceed.

Diversity of Thought and Background

The project seeks complementary strengths rather than identical perspectives.

Examples include:

  • ANHQV continuity specialists;
  • people with precise memory who are willing to verify it;
  • simmers and builders;
  • experimentalists;
  • sceptics;
  • video editors and visual communicators;
  • reverse engineers and software developers;
  • archivists and rights researchers;
  • historians and cultural researchers;
  • documentation specialists;
  • translators and accessibility contributors;
  • people who ask unusually productive questions.

Homogeneity is a long-term risk.

Evidence Before Consensus

Recruitment does not imply trust in a contributor's conclusions.

It implies trust in their process and conduct.

Every contributor is expected to challenge assumptions—including those of moderators—when supported by reasoning or evidence.

Disagreement should normally produce:

  • discussion;
  • refinement;
  • experiment;
  • source review;
  • documentation.

Not conflict.

Public Participation and Formal Recruitment

Anyone may propose a question, correction, source, experiment, or pull request.

Formal mission roles and permissions require documented recruitment and onboarding.

Accepting a useful one-off contribution does not automatically assign a formal role, and a candidate does not need to know every aspect of the project before being considered.

Recruitment Workflow

Candidate identified
        ↓
Mission role or specialist profile selected
        ↓
Interview conducted
        ↓
Evaluation completed
        ↓
Second-moderator review
        ↓
Decision recorded
        ↓
Onboarding
        ↓
First contribution and review

Two live interviewers are preferred when practical. When only one moderator attends live, a Reviewing Moderator independently reviews the interview record and recommendation.

No formal contributor bypasses evaluation.

No decision bypasses documentation.

Recruitment as Research

Recruitment itself remains open to improvement.

If evidence shows that interviews, criteria, or onboarding fail to identify suitable collaborators, they should be revised through:

  • observation;
  • discussion;
  • documented reasoning;
  • review;
  • implementation;
  • later evaluation.

Success

Recruitment is successful when a contributor consistently improves the project without compromising its principles.

The process should be measured not by how many people join, but by how much stronger, more accurate, more welcoming, and more durable the project becomes after they do.
