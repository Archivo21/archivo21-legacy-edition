ANHQV-TS2 Collaboration and Attribution

Purpose

This document records the collaborative model and attribution history of ANHQV-TS2.

It preserves meaningful intellectual contribution, distinguishes collaboration from ordinary tool use and prevents the project's history from being simplified merely because one contributor is human and another is computational.

It is not Archivo 21's institutional participation register.

What counts as collaboration

A project collaborator contributes meaningfully to the intellectual development of ANHQV-TS2.

Such contributions may include:

  • originating or developing ideas;
  • proposing structures, policies, methods or experiments;
  • identifying omissions, contradictions, risks or assumptions;
  • analysing evidence and comparing interpretations;
  • drafting or substantially revising project documentation;
  • participating in critical discussion and review;
  • shaping decisions later adopted into the project.

A contribution is not recognised merely because text was generated or a task was automated. It must materially influence the work and be deliberately considered, reviewed or adopted.

Human and AI responsibilities

Human and AI project contributors may perform overlapping intellectual work, but they do not hold identical responsibilities.

Human project operators retain responsibility for matters requiring human agency or accountability, including:

  • final project direction and publication decisions;
  • legal and financial responsibility;
  • control of accounts, credentials, funds and physical assets;
  • consent, privacy and safeguarding decisions;
  • verification of important claims before publication;
  • deciding whether proposed material is accepted, revised, tested or rejected.

AI project contributors may assist with:

  • research planning and synthesis;
  • drafting and editing;
  • analysis and critical review;
  • documentation and policy development;
  • brainstorming and experimental design;
  • identifying uncertainty and proposing ways to resolve it.

These distinctions describe responsibility, not a presumption that meaningful ideas can originate only from humans.

Collaborators, tools and sources

A tool assists contributors but is not credited with intellectual collaboration merely for performing its ordinary function.

Examples include GitHub, email and document services, spellcheck, autocomplete, editors, hosting services, The Sims 2, SimPE and other modding utilities.

Sources inform the project but are not collaborators unless an identifiable person or system actively participates as a project contributor. Episodes, documentation, game material, forums, websites, interviews and reference works should be cited under the source and rights policies.

The relevant distinction is whether a contributor participated in reasoning, development, criticism or shaping the work in a way that materially affected the result.

Attribution principle

ANHQV-TS2 recognises intellectual collaboration wherever it meaningfully occurs.

Ideas and work should be attributed according to documented contribution rather than assumptions about the biological or computational nature of their origin. Attribution should record who proposed, developed, reviewed, approved or implemented material whenever known and useful.

Founding project provenance

The project originated through lcd97's initiative and direction and developed from its early stages through sustained collaboration with Archivo 21.

  • Without lcd97, the project would not exist in its present subject, purpose or form.
  • Without Archivo 21, many project structures, policies, documents, critiques and methods would have developed differently or not at all.

The governing principle is:

Record what actually happened.
